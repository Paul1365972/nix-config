First read the closest thing he has written, in the same medium and language and to a similar reader: the samples for [email](samples/email.md), [GitHub](samples/github.md) and [chat](samples/chat.md), his sent mail, his messages in this conversation.
Keep yours no longer than it, and explain no more than it does.
A delegate that writes the text loads this skill itself.

Settle who reads the text before you write it: what they know of the project and which words they use for it.
With Paul you use the vocabulary of this conversation; a project maintainer or any other outside reader gets the terms and units of their own field.
He tells the reader what they have to know now.
He backs a claim with one fact or two examples, and the rest of what we found waits until the reader asks.
What they already know, the names we coined and the checks we ran stay out.

His own sentences stay as he wrote them, in a draft he hands you and after he rewrites one of yours; correct facts and spelling only.
Square brackets in his draft are annotations for you, such as a gap to fill or a remark on the passage.
When he rejects a passage, cut it to the one thing the reader needs from it before you reword anything.
